A Practical Guide to Starting a Business

When coming up with an idea, it should be detailed and practical—something that someone can take and start making cash with. Before you jump in, it's crucial to vet your idea and understand the full scope of the commitment.

1. The Initial Litmus Test: Vetting Your Idea

  • Feasibility: In this, we check on all potential costs. Is it realistically possible to build and operate this business?
  • Economic Viability: The business should withstand current and future economic changes.
  • Future-Proofing: Capability of handling all financial and economic shifts in the future.
  • Legality: The business idea should only be legal.
  • Primary Aim: Is it for service provision or for profit-making?
  • Minimum Legal Requirements: Licenses, permits, and regulations (e.g., CCTV for cyber cafes).

2. Business Selection Criteria

Market and Competitive Factors

  • Market Demand: Is there a clear, ongoing need for your product?
  • Market Size & Growth: Is the market large enough to support growth?
  • Target Audience: Is there a clear and specific group of customers?
  • Niche & Competitive Advantage: What makes this business unique? (USP).

3. Wealth Creation: Why Invest Rather Than Save?

Saving is keeping money for future use, but Investing is putting your money to work to generate more money. Over time, inflation reduces the value of savings, while investments can grow faster than inflation.

  • Buying Shares (Equity): When you buy shares, you own a "piece" of a company. You benefit from dividends (profit sharing) and capital appreciation (the share price going up).
  • The Power of Compounding: Investing early allows your returns to earn their own returns. This is how small amounts grow into large fortunes over time.
  • Risk Management: Unlike saving in a bank, shares carry risk. However, diversifying (buying different types of shares) helps protect your capital.
  • Accessibility: In 2026, you can start buying shares via mobile apps with as little as 1000/=.

5. Print-on-Demand: Printify & Etsy Guide

This is a low-capital digital business that allows you to sell custom products globally.

  • Sign up: Create a free Printify account and select "Artist/Designer".
  • Connect Etsy: Link your Etsy shop to Printify for automated order fulfillment.
  • Product Design: Upload your designs to products; Printify handles the printing and shipping.
  • Publishing: Once you publish from Printify, the items appear live on your Etsy shop.
