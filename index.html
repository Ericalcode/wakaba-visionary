A Practical Guide to Starting a Business

When coming up with an idea, it should be detailed and practical—something that someone can take and start making cash with. Before you jump in, it's crucial to vet your idea and understand the full scope of the commitment.

1. The Initial Litmus Test: Vetting Your Idea

  • Feasibility: In this, we check on all potential costs. Is it realistically possible to build and operate this business?
  • Economic Viability: The business should withstand current and future economic changes.
  • Future-Proofing: Capability of handling all financial and economic shifts in the future.
  • Legality: The business idea should only be legal.
  • Primary Aim: Is it for service provision or for profit-making?
  • Minimum Legal Requirements: Licenses, permits, and regulations (e.g., CCTV for cyber cafes).

2. Business Selection Criteria

Market and Competitive Factors

  • Market Demand: Is there a clear, ongoing need for your product?
  • Market Size & Growth: Is the market large enough to support growth?
  • Target Audience: Is there a clear and specific group of customers?
  • Niche & Competitive Advantage: What makes this business unique? (USP).

Financial and Operational Factors

  • Startup Costs & Funding: What are initial costs and the funding plan?
  • Profitability Potential: Healthy margins and a realistic break-even timeline.
  • Location and Accessibility: Is the location strategic or remote-friendly?

3. Planning for Success, Profits, and Expenses

  • Scalability: Can the business grow revenue without proportional cost increases?
  • Revenue Streams: How will you make money? (Daily sales, subs, etc.)
  • Fixed Costs: Consistent expenses like rent and salaries.
  • Variable Costs: Costs that fluctuate with sales (inventory, transport).

4. Capital Investment and Funding

Advantages of External Finance: Access to large capital, growth enablement, and risk sharing.

Critical Funding Mistakes to Avoid: Mixing expenses, undercapitalization, and premature scaling.

5. Business Focus: Fuerte & Hass Avocado Trade

Market Intel (Updated Jan 25, 2026 - James): The business performs exceptionally well from April onwards.

Market Dynamics:

  • Varieties: Fuerte (Starting field price: 12/= per kg) and Hass (Approx. 15/= per kg).
  • Primary Market: Thika, which hosts at least 3 major factories.
  • Factory Selling Price: Starts at approx. 24/= per kg (Minimum floor price usually 21/=).

The Operational Math (Single Trip Example):

Expense Item Amount (KES)
Purchase (2500kg x 12/=) 30,000
Transport (Field to Thika - e.g. Isuzu D-Max) 5,000
Labour (3 people for harvest/loading) 3,000
Road Transport Fee 150
Total Investment 38,150
Factory Sale (2500kg x 24/=) 60,000
Profit per Trip ~21,850

Execution Steps:

  • Field Search: Locate trees, bargain price (Target: < 12/=per kg).
  • Logistics: A standard pickup/D-Max can carry ~2800kg. Collect up to 2500kg per day.
  • Frequency: With good coordination, you can achieve 3 trips per week.
  • Requirements: 50k starting capital, sacks, weighing machine, and transport permits.
